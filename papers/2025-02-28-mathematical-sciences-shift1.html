ID 1Q1. If a map is placed in such a manner that southwest becomes east, then what will north become?
  1. Northeast
  2. Southwest
  3. Northwest
  4. Southeast
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (2)

What is given. The map is rotated rigidly, and under that rotation the southwest direction ends up pointing east.
Idea used. Rotating a map turns every direction by the same angle. Put the eight compass points on a circle at $45^\circ$ spacing, find the single angle that carries SW to E, then apply it to N.
  1. Put numbers on the compass. Measuring clockwise from north: N $=0^\circ$, NE $=45^\circ$, E $=90^\circ$, SE $=135^\circ$, S $=180^\circ$, SW $=225^\circ$, W $=270^\circ$, NW $=315^\circ$.
  2. Find the rotation. SW sits at $225^\circ$ and must land on E at $90^\circ$, so the rotation $\theta$ satisfies $225^\circ+\theta\equiv 90^\circ \pmod{360^\circ}$, giving $\theta = -135^\circ$, that is $225^\circ$ clockwise.
  3. Apply it to north. N is at $0^\circ$, so it moves to $0^\circ+225^\circ = 225^\circ$.
  4. Read the answer off the compass. $225^\circ$ is southwest.

North becomes southwest, option (2).

Common slip. Rotating by the wrong amount because SW and E look like a $180^\circ$ flip. They are $135^\circ$ apart, not $180^\circ$, so the two directions are not opposite and the naive answer northeast is wrong.

ID 2Q2. If water of pH 8 is diluted 100 times with neutral water (pH $=7$) then it will
  1. become acidic.
  2. remain basic.
  3. become neutral.
  4. become heavy.
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (2)

What is given. A solution at pH $8$, so mildly basic, diluted a hundredfold with water that is exactly neutral.
Idea used. Dilution moves a solution towards neutrality but can never carry it past neutrality. Adding neutral water removes no excess hydroxide, it only spreads it through more volume, so the excess shrinks towards zero and pH approaches $7$ from above without reaching it.
  1. Read the starting concentrations. pH $8$ means $[\mathrm{H^+}]=10^{-8}$ and, from $[\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]=10^{-14}$, $[\mathrm{OH^-}]=10^{-6}$ mol/L.
  2. Identify what actually gets diluted. What makes the solution basic is the excess of hydroxide over the neutral value, namely $10^{-6}-10^{-7}=9\times10^{-7}$ mol/L. Diluting a hundredfold leaves an excess of $9\times10^{-9}$ mol/L.
  3. Solve for the new pH. With $h=[\mathrm{H^+}]$, charge balance gives $\dfrac{10^{-14}}{h}-h = 9\times10^{-9}$, that is $h^{2}+9\times10^{-9}h-10^{-14}=0$, whose positive root is $h\approx 9.56\times10^{-8}$.
  4. Convert back. $\mathrm{pH} = -\log_{10}(9.56\times10^{-8}) \approx 7.02$, which is above $7$.
  5. Interpret. The solution has moved very close to neutral but is still on the basic side, and no amount of neutral water will push it below $7$.

It remains basic, option (2).

Common slip. Subtracting $\log_{10}100 = 2$ from the pH to get $6$ and calling the result acidic. pH is not additive under dilution like that; treating it so would let you turn a base into an acid with pure water.

ID 3Q3. Fifteen distinct points are randomly placed on the circumference of a circle. How many distinct straight lines at the most can be formed by pairs among these points?
  1. 105
  2. 455
  3. 30
  4. 210
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (1)

What is given. Fifteen distinct points, all lying on one circle.
Idea used. Two points determine one line, so the count is a combination. The circle matters because no three points of a circle are ever collinear, so no two different pairs give the same line.
  1. Count the pairs. A line needs two points, and order does not matter, so the number of pairs is $$\binom{15}{2}=\frac{15\times14}{2}=105.$$
  2. Check that the lines are distinct. A straight line meets a circle in at most two points, so no three of the fifteen points are collinear. Different pairs therefore give different lines and there is no over-counting.
  3. Conclude. All $105$ lines are distinct, so $105$ is both the maximum and the exact count.

There are 105 distinct lines, option (1).

Common slip. Using $\binom{15}{3}=455$, which counts triangles rather than lines, or $15\times 14 = 210$, which counts ordered pairs and so counts every line twice.

ID 4Q4. In a population of microbial cells, the initial population is $50$, and the growth rate is $0.1$ per hour. If the population grows exponentially, what will be the approximate size of the population after $10$ hours?
  1. 51
  2. 82
  3. 136
  4. 156
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (3)

What is given. $N_0 = 50$ cells, continuous growth rate $r = 0.1$ per hour, elapsed time $t = 10$ hours.
Idea used. Exponential growth at a continuous rate $r$ obeys $N(t) = N_0 e^{rt}$. The product $rt$ here is exactly $1$, which makes the arithmetic a single multiplication by $e$.
  1. Write the model. $N(t) = N_0 e^{rt}$.
  2. Substitute. $N(10) = 50\,e^{0.1\times 10} = 50\,e^{1}$.
  3. Evaluate. $e \approx 2.71828$, so $N(10) \approx 50\times 2.71828 = 135.9$.
  4. Round. Cells are whole, so the population is about $136$.

The population is approximately 136, option (3).

Common slip. Using simple growth, $50(1+0.1\times 10) = 100$, or compounding discretely as $50(1.1)^{10}\approx 130$. The phrase “grows exponentially” with a rate per hour means the continuous model $e^{rt}$.

ID 5Q5. The given figure shows data points and a line fit by the least squares method between profit in an ice-cream business and mean temperature $T$ for a city. The fitted line is $\text{Profit} = 4T - 86$ and the correlation coefficient is $r = 0.83$. Which one of the following inferences can definitely be drawn?
  1. The sum of the squared values of differences between the observed and expected values of temperature is the minimum.
  2. $83\%$ of the variation in profit is explained by the variation in temperature.
  3. Rise in temperature causes profit to increase.
  4. At $25^\circ$C, estimated profit is $14$ million ₹.
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (4)

What is given. A least-squares line $\text{Profit}=4T-86$ with profit in millions of rupees and $T$ in degrees Celsius, together with $r=0.83$.
Idea used. Only what the fitted equation itself asserts is safe. Least squares minimises residuals in the response, the proportion of variance explained is $r^2$ and not $r$, and correlation never establishes causation.
  1. Test option (4) by substituting. $\text{Profit}=4(25)-86 = 100-86 = 14$ million rupees. This is exactly what the fitted line predicts at $T=25$, so the statement is true by arithmetic.
  2. Reject option (1). Ordinary least squares minimises $\sum(y_i-\hat y_i)^2$, the squared deviations in profit, the dependent variable. The statement refers to deviations in temperature, which the fit does not minimise.
  3. Reject option (2). The fraction of variation explained is the coefficient of determination $r^{2}=(0.83)^{2}\approx 0.69$, so about $69\%$, not $83\%$.
  4. Reject option (3). A correlation of $0.83$ shows the two move together, but the data are observational. Hot weather and ice-cream profit could share a common cause, and nothing in a regression line rules that out.

Only the prediction at $25^\circ$C follows definitely, option (4).

Common slip. Reading $r=0.83$ as “$83\%$ explained”. The explained fraction is $r^2$, which here is about $69\%$; the two agree only when $r$ is $0$ or $1$.

ID 6Q6. The average of seven numbers is $71$. If we exclude one of these numbers, the average becomes $75$. What is that number?
  1. 75
  2. 74
  3. 73
  4. 47
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (4)

What is given. Seven numbers averaging $71$; after one is removed the remaining six average $75$.
Idea used. Convert each average into a total. The removed number is simply the difference of the two totals.
  1. Total of all seven. $7\times 71 = 497$.
  2. Total of the remaining six. $6\times 75 = 450$.
  3. Subtract. The excluded number is $497-450 = 47$.
  4. Check. Removing $47$, a number well below the old average, should pull the average up, and it does: from $71$ to $75$.

The number is 47, option (4).

Common slip. Expecting the answer to sit near $71$ or $75$ and picking $73$. Removing one value from seven moves the mean a long way, so the removed value must be far from both averages.

ID 7Q7. Choose the option to fill in the blank that will make the following statement logically correct:

THE NUMBER OF OCCURRENCES OF THE LETTER “N” IN THIS SENTENCE IS CORRECTLY COUNTED AS ________ .
  1. SIX
  2. SEVEN
  3. EIGHT
  4. NINE
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (4)

What is given. A self-referential sentence: the word placed in the blank becomes part of the sentence being counted.
Idea used. Count the N's in the fixed part once, then test each candidate, remembering that the candidate word contributes its own N's to the total it claims.
  1. Count the N's in the fixed part. NUMBER contributes $1$, OCCURRENCES $1$, the quoted letter “N” $1$, IN $1$, SENTENCE $2$, COUNTED $1$. That is $7$ before the blank is filled.
  2. Test SIX. SIX contains no N, so the total stays $7$, but the sentence claims $6$. False.
  3. Test SEVEN. SEVEN contains one N, so the total becomes $8$, while the sentence claims $7$. False.
  4. Test EIGHT. EIGHT contains no N, so the total stays $7$, while the sentence claims $8$. False.
  5. Test NINE. NINE contains two N's, so the total becomes $7+2 = 9$, and the sentence claims $9$. True.

The blank must read NINE, option (4).

Common slip. Counting the fixed part, getting $7$, and answering SEVEN. Writing SEVEN into the blank adds an N of its own and makes the count $8$, so the sentence refutes itself.

ID 8Q8. In a board meeting of $20$ directors, $6$ shook everyone else’s hands but the remaining $14$ did not shake each another’s. The total number of handshakes in the meeting was
  1. 26
  2. 84
  3. 99
  4. 190
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (3)

What is given. Twenty people. Six of them shook hands with all nineteen others. The other fourteen shook hands only with those six, never among themselves.
Idea used. Split the handshakes into three groups by who is involved: both from the sociable six, one from each side, or both from the fourteen. Count each group separately so no handshake is counted twice.
  1. Within the six. Every pair among the six shook hands: $\binom{6}{2}=15$.
  2. Between the six and the fourteen. Each of the six shook hands with each of the fourteen: $6\times 14 = 84$.
  3. Within the fourteen. None, by hypothesis: $0$.
  4. Add. $15+84+0 = 99$.

There were 99 handshakes, option (3).

Common slip. Answering $6\times 19 = 114$ or $\binom{20}{2}=190$. The first double-counts the handshakes among the six; the second assumes everyone shook everyone's hand, which the fourteen did not.

ID 9Q9. Choose the correct chronological order of the following:
A: match,   B: trophy,   C: toss,   D: result.
  1. C, A, D, B
  2. A, D, B, C
  3. C, B, A, D
  4. D, C, B, A
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (1)

What is given. Four stages of a sporting fixture, to be put in the order in which they occur.
Idea used. Order the events by what each one depends on: the toss decides how the match starts, the result exists only once the match is over, and the trophy is awarded on the strength of the result.
  1. The toss comes first. It is held before play begins, so C leads.
  2. Then the match. Play follows the toss: A.
  3. Then the result. A result exists only after the match finishes: D.
  4. The trophy last. It is presented on the basis of the result: B.

The order is C, A, D, B, option (1).

Common slip. Placing the trophy before the result because it feels like the climax of the event. The presentation depends on the result, so it cannot precede it.

ID 10Q10. A spherical ball is placed inside a cubic box. If the diameter of the ball is same as the sides of the box, what approximate percentage of volume will be empty?
  1. $12\%$
  2. $24\%$
  3. $36\%$
  4. $48\%$
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (4)

What is given. A sphere inscribed in a cube, so the sphere's diameter equals the cube's edge.
Idea used. Take the edge as $a$, compute both volumes in terms of $a$, and the ratio is a pure number: the $a^3$ cancels, so the answer does not depend on the size of the box.
  1. Volume of the cube. With edge $a$, it is $a^{3}$.
  2. Volume of the ball. The diameter is $a$, so the radius is $a/2$ and $$V_{\text{sphere}}=\frac{4}{3}\pi\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^{3}=\frac{\pi a^{3}}{6}.$$
  3. Fraction occupied. $\dfrac{V_{\text{sphere}}}{V_{\text{cube}}}=\dfrac{\pi}{6}\approx 0.5236$, about $52.4\%$.
  4. Fraction empty. $1-\dfrac{\pi}{6}\approx 0.4764$, that is about $48\%$.

About $\mathbf{48\%}$ of the box is empty, option (4).

Common slip. Using the radius as $a$ instead of $a/2$, which makes the sphere larger than the box. The diameter equals the edge, so the radius is half of it.

ID 11Q11. If I walked east $100$ metres, turned right and walked $60$ metres, turned left and walked $150$ metres and turned left again, I would be facing
  1. East
  2. North
  3. West
  4. South
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (2)

What is given. A walk starting eastward, with a right turn, then a left turn, then another left turn. Turns are quarter-turns.
Idea used. Only the turns matter; the distances are irrelevant to the final heading. Track the facing direction one turn at a time.
  1. Start. Facing east.
  2. Turn right. From east, a right turn faces south. Walk $60$ m.
  3. Turn left. From south, a left turn faces east again. Walk $150$ m.
  4. Turn left again. From east, a left turn faces north.

I would be facing north, option (2).

Common slip. Trying to track the position as well and getting lost in the arithmetic. The question asks only for the heading, and the three distances never enter the answer.

ID 12Q12. The words TEST, EXAM and EAST are coded as $1382$, $2182$ and $1937$ but not necessarily in that order. How would the word MATE be coded?
  1. 9321
  2. 7321
  3. 7312
  4. 1982
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (2)

What is given. Three four-letter words and three four-digit codes, with the pairing unknown.
Idea used. Match on structure before matching on letters. TEST is the only word with a repeated letter, and it repeats in the first and fourth positions, so its code must repeat a digit in exactly those places.
  1. Use the repetition pattern. TEST has T in positions $1$ and $4$. Among the codes, only $2182$ has equal first and last digits. Hence TEST $=2182$, giving $\mathrm{T}=2$, $\mathrm{E}=1$, $\mathrm{S}=8$.
  2. Place EAST. With $\mathrm{E}=1$, $\mathrm{S}=8$, $\mathrm{T}=2$, the code for EAST must read $1\,?\,8\,2$. That matches $1382$, so $\mathrm{A}=3$.
  3. Confirm with EXAM. The remaining code is $1937$, and EXAM must start with $\mathrm{E}=1$ and have $\mathrm{A}=3$ in the third place. $1937$ fits exactly, giving $\mathrm{X}=9$ and $\mathrm{M}=7$.
  4. Encode MATE. $\mathrm{M}=7$, $\mathrm{A}=3$, $\mathrm{T}=2$, $\mathrm{E}=1$, so MATE $=7321$.

MATE is coded 7321, option (2).

Common slip. Assuming the codes are listed in the same order as the words. The question says otherwise, and pairing TEST with $1382$ makes the whole substitution inconsistent.

ID 13Q13. All those who pass an entrance test take admission into a certain institute. Out of these, some graduate with a degree in $2$ years while some fail and are removed, and all graduates from that institute get jobs in the same year. In $2022$, no one took admission in that institute. Which of the following does NOT follow necessarily?
  1. No one wrote the entrance test in 2022
  2. No one passed the entrance test in 2022
  3. No one graduated from the institute in 2024
  4. No one got a job from the institute in 2024
Show step-by-step solution

Answer: (1)

What is given. Passing implies admission; a degree takes two years; every graduate is employed in the year of graduating; and there were no admissions in $2022$.
Idea used. Each true statement here comes from a contrapositive. Writing the test is upstream of passing, and nothing in the premises links writing to admission, so that is the one that escapes.
  1. Statement (2) follows. “Passes $\Rightarrow$ admitted” contraposes to “not admitted $\Rightarrow$ did not pass”. With no admissions in $2022$, nobody passed in $2022$.
  2. Statement (3) follows. A degree takes two years, so a $2024$ graduate must have been admitted in $2022$. There were no such admissions, so there are no $2024$ graduates.
  3. Statement (4) follows. Jobs come only through graduating, in the same year. With no graduates in $2024$, nobody got a job from the institute that year.
  4. Statement (1) does not follow. Any number of candidates could have written the test in $2022$ and every one of them failed. Failing is consistent with no admissions, so the premises say nothing about who sat the test.

Statement (1) is the one that does not follow, option (1).

Common slip. Reading “no one took admission” as “nothing happened”. The premises constrain only what follows after passing; the act of writing the test is left completely open.
