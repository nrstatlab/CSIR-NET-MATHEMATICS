What this site is

A free, growing archive of CSIR NET Mathematical Sciences question papers in which every question is worked out, not merely answered. The aim is that an aspirant who has never seen a question before can read the solution once and know both the result and the reason for it.

Each question is typeset with MathJax rather than pasted in as a screenshot. That means it renders sharply on a phone, it can be copied into your own notes, and a search engine can actually find it. Under each question a single click opens the full solution.

How each solution is laid out:
  • What is given — the data of the problem restated in clean notation.
  • Idea used — the theorem or technique the setter is testing, named explicitly.
  • Steps 1, 2, 3 … — the working, one numbered step at a time, nothing skipped.
  • Conclusion — the correct option, and why the others fail.
  • Common slip — the mistake that makes candidates choose the wrong option.

Duration is three hours. In Part C one or more options may be correct and marks are awarded only when every correct option is selected and no incorrect one is — there is no partial credit, but also no penalty for a wrong attempt.

If more than the required number are attempted, the examination system evaluates only the first correctly-numbered responses up to the limit for that part. Mark your intended answers deliberately rather than filling every question.

Papers are added continuously

This archive grows one paper at a time. A question paper PDF dropped into the repository joins the queue, gets transcribed into MathJax, is solved, checked, and then published on its own page. Nothing appears here until its solution has been worked through and verified, so a large paper often lands in instalments.

The whole procedure, including where to put a new PDF, is written up in How to add a paper.
